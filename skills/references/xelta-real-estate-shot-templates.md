# Real Estate Shot Templates

Six shot types agents ask for on repeat. Use one as a scaffold under whichever
house style is active (see `SKILL.md` → "Brand/style consistency"), then
apply the basic/detailed/cinematic expansion level on top.

## Exterior establishing / curb appeal

- **Subject** — full front facade, driveway/entry, landscaping.
- **Framing** — wide, eye-level or a slight low angle.
- **Lighting** — bright midday or soft morning light, unless the active
  house style overrides it.
- **Cinematic add** — slow push-in toward the entry, or a static hold with
  subtle handheld drift.

## Interior room-by-room walkthrough

- **Subject** — one room per shot, entry point to far wall.
- **Framing** — eye-level, wide enough to read the whole room.
- **Lighting** — even ambient fill; avoid blown-out windows.
- **Cinematic add** — slow lateral dolly, or a forward walk from the
  doorway.

## Kitchen/bathroom close-up detail

- **Subject** — countertop/island, fixtures, finishes — not the whole room.
- **Framing** — medium-close, 3/4 angle to show material texture.
- **Lighting** — bright and even, minimal shadow so finishes read clearly.
- **Cinematic add** — slow rack focus from a foreground detail to the room
  behind it.

## Aerial/drone property overview

- **Subject** — full lot, roofline, surrounding context.
- **Framing** — high angle, straight-down or 45°, wide.
- **Lighting** — bright and clear, minimal haze.
- **Cinematic add** — slow orbit, or a rise reveal.

## "Day to dusk" twilight exterior

- **Subject** — front or rear facade with interior lights visible through
  windows.
- **Framing** — same wide composition as the curb-appeal shot, held through
  the light transition.
- **Lighting** — blue-hour sky with warm interior glow through windows; the
  contrast between the two is the point of the shot.
- **Cinematic add** — static hold or slow push-in as interior light reads
  warmer against the cooling sky.

## Before/after renovation reveal

- **Subject** — same framing and angle for both states so the comparison
  reads clearly.
- **Framing** — lock the composition; angle and distance must match between
  before and after.
- **Lighting** — match as closely as possible between the two shots so the
  renovation is the only variable that changed.
- **Cinematic add** — hard cut or quick wipe between the two states, not a
  slow dissolve.
